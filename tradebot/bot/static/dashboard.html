    How the bot reads a chart

    BUY: breakout
    A tight range (consolidation), then a candle closes above it on 2x+ normal volume, above VWAP. Stop under the range.
    BUY: pullback
    Higher highs and higher lows, price dips into the 21 EMA, VWAP or the 0.382-0.618 Fibonacci zone, then a hammer or bullish engulfing candle. Stop under the dip.
    BUY: VWAP reclaim
    Price was under VWAP and closes back above it on 1.5x volume. Stop under the reclaim candle.
    SHORT (Liquid perps only)
    The same rules on the chart turned upside down: a breakdown below a range, a bounce into the 21 EMA/VWAP in a downtrend that gets rejected, or losing VWAP. Stops sit above.
    NEVER BUY
    More than 30% above VWAP (chasing), RSI over 80, a long upper wick (sellers), bearish divergence, a downtrend, or a token that fails the rug checks.
    SELL in steps
    Half at +1R (R = entry minus stop), a quarter at +2R. After +1R the stop goes to break-even and then trails under each new higher low.
    SELL everything when
    The last higher low breaks, a candle closes under the 21 EMA after a good move, a shooting star or bearish engulfing appears on volume at the highs, RSI diverges, volume dries up, or 4 hours pass.

    What do these mean?

    Backtest: runs the strategy with its current settings over the history. Walk-forward: picks the best settings on one stretch of history, then trades them on the next stretch it never saw, and repeats. Only the out-of-sample (unseen) result tells you much about the future. Compare: every strategy on the same data next to buy-and-hold. A great backtest that fails walk-forward is usually curve-fitted.

    Simple formula: ((1-A)/(1+A))^U, only valid when wins and losses are the same size. Ralph Vince's formula works for any win rate and uneven wins/losses. Under 1% is comfortable; above 5% means size down.

    Before you switch to live money

    1. Run the walk-forward test for every strategy you turn on. Only use ones rated ROBUST or MODERATE.
    2. Paper trade for 1 to 3 months (walk-forward video) and compare the results above with the backtest. Big gaps mean something is off.
    3. Check the risk of ruin with your real paper results. Keep it under 1% for the loss you could live with.
    4. Start live with small sizes: lower "max position" and "risk per trade" in Settings.
    5. Never put more in the bot's wallet than you can afford to lose. Memecoins can go to zero in minutes.

    Connect FOMO

    1. In the FOMO app: Settings → Account → Export (Face ID). Copy the Solana private key (about 88 characters), not the 0x one.
    2. Paste it into FOMO_WALLET_PRIVATE_KEY above, save, then press Test my connections. Check the wallet address matches the one in FOMO. The key stays on this computer.
    3. Keep some USDC (what the bot spends) in the wallet and send about 0.05 SOL for network fees: FOMO pays gas only inside its own app, and selling needs SOL.
    4. Optional: a free Jupiter API key from developers.jup.ag/portal as JUPITER_API_KEY (faster), and a private Solana RPC such as Helius (more reliable).
    5. The coins the bot buys appear in your FOMO holdings; FOMO's own history may not list them, the Trade journal here does. The bot only ever sells what it bought.
    Anyone with this key can take everything in the wallet. Never paste it into a chat, a website or a message. Consider a separate FOMO account just for the bot.

    Connect Liquid

    1. Liquid trades on Hyperliquid. Open app.hyperliquid.xyz → More → API, connect the same wallet your Liquid account uses, and create an API wallet. It can trade but can never withdraw.
    2. Paste the API wallet's private key into HL_API_PRIVATE_KEY, and your account address (Liquid shows it, starts with 0x) into HL_ACCOUNT_ADDRESS.
    3. Stock markets like NVDA trade as xyz:NVDA and may need USDC moved to that market in Liquid first.

    Connect TradingView

    1. Pick any password and save it as TV_WEBHOOK_SECRET above.
    2. Make the webhook port reachable from the internet with a free tunnel, e.g. cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:8788. Use the https address it prints, plus /webhook. Never tunnel the dashboard's port.
    3. In TradingView: create an alert → Notifications → Webhook URL, and paste this as the message:
    
        
    For a Pine strategy, use "action": "{{strategy.order.action}}", "position": "{{strategy.market_position}}". The ready-made strategy tradingview/tradebot_strategy.pine sends these for you. TradingView webhooks need a paid TradingView plan. Practise with TradingView's own Strategy Tester first, and keep the bot in paper mode while you test alerts.
