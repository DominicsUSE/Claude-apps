Version 1, 23 November 2026. Everything here is built to be used in the next production session: the engine is a fill-in template, the scripts are shootable, the checklists are tick boxes.

## 1. The comedy formula (what to steal: the mechanics, never the material)

### Why adult animated satire lands

**Speed makes it feel immediate.** The famous example is a show that writes, animates and delivers an episode in about six days, so it can react to the news of that week. You will never match a studio's craft. You can match its turnaround. A 30-second cartoon about a thing people argued about on Monday, published Friday, beats a polished one published a month later.

**"But" and "therefore", never "and then".** The creators of the best-known show in this genre have described their rule in public: every beat must connect to the next with *but* or *therefore*. If you can join two beats with "and then", the second beat is random and the joke dies. This is why absurd escalation feels inevitable instead of silly. It is the single most useful rule in this document.

**Childish art carries adult ideas past people's defences.** Crude visuals lower the stakes, so a harsh observation reads as a cartoon instead of a lecture. The contrast is itself a joke: a lumpy drawing delivering a precise point about hypocrisy.

**The target is behaviour, not identity.** The genre mocks hypocrisy, overreaction, moral panic, grift and institutional cowardice. Episodes often mock both sides of a fight and then the person who thinks they are above it. Taboo topics work when the taboo is the *subject* of the joke, never the punchline itself.

**Fixed characters are lenses.** Each character reacts to every situation the same way: the schemer schemes, the moralist moralises, the oblivious one misses it. The audience can predict the type of reaction but never the specific one. Comedy comes from these lenses colliding.

**Total commitment.** Characters take insane premises seriously. Nobody winks. Deadpan delivery of a ridiculous line is funnier than a "zany" one.

**A clear point, mocked as it is delivered.** Each story has one observation underneath it. The genre often ends with a character summarising the lesson, and the summary itself is part of the joke.

**Rewatchability comes from density.** Background signs, quotable lines, a callback to an earlier episode. On Shorts, the equivalent is the loop: a final frame that drops the viewer back into frame one.

### Funny vs merely offensive or random

A joke is funny when four things hold:

1. **The butt has agency.** The joke lands on a person holding a bad idea, an institution, or a behaviour. If the punchline *is* someone's race, disability, religion, gender or sexuality, it is not satire, it is a slur with timing.
2. **The logic holds.** Every escalation follows from the previous step (but/therefore). Random = the audience stops caring because nothing they predict matters.
3. **It is specific.** "Phones are bad" is a TED talk. "Scrolling is listed as her most-used app" is a joke.
4. **It turns.** The last beat reverses, recontextualises or calls back. Shock without a turn is just shock.

### Hard lines for this channel

- **No real people.** No real politicians, celebrities, creators or private citizens, by name, likeness, voice or obvious stand-in. Satirise the behaviour ("a council that rebrands instead of fixing"), not the person.
- **No real brands, logos or apps.** Every app, store and device in town is fictional (Porchlight, PeepHole Pro, ValuMore). This also kills defamation risk: you never state something false about a real company.
- **No factual claims as fact.** If a Short references a statistic, it is a fictional one inside the town (a fake listicle site, a fake news chyron).
- **No punching down.** Never target poverty, disability, immigrants, minorities, or victims. Punch at power, vanity, grift and institutions. Gord is old, and the jokes are about his choices, never his age as a defect.
- **No harassment.** No Short "about" a real creator, comment, or viral person.
- **No sexual content involving anyone under 18.** The cast is all adults (youngest is 19) partly for this reason.
- **No voice cloning of any real person**, including your friends without written consent.

### The reusable comedy engine

Fill this in before writing a single line of dialogue. If any field is blank, the Short is not ready.

| Field | Question | Pothole example (Short 1) |
|---|---|---|
| **Core premise** | What tiny, relatable problem starts it? | The town's pothole is about to be repaired. |
| **Central contradiction** | What do people say vs what do they do? | Residents complained about it for years, but now it is part of their identity. |
| **Escalation ladder** | 3 to 5 rungs, each joined by *but/therefore* | Deb posts outrage, *therefore* Marcus sells merch, *therefore* Wendy declares it heritage, *therefore* Gord blocks the crew, *but* the rich town next door fills it overnight. |
| **Comic target** | Who or what is the joke really on? | Communities that defend their problems once the problem becomes a brand. |
| **Emotional reaction** | What should the viewer feel at the end? | Recognition ("my town would do this") plus a guilty laugh. |
| **Final punchline / reversal** | How does the last beat flip or loop? | The raccoon digs a new hole and lights the candle: loops to frame one. |
| **The point (one sentence, never spoken aloud)** | What is the observation? | We will protect anything that makes us feel like a community, including a hole. |

**Shorts compression rules**

- 30 seconds holds 70 to 90 spoken words at comedy pace with pauses. Over 95 words means you have cut no pauses, and pauses are where the laughs are.
- Exactly three escalation beats on screen. Five rungs are fine if two are visual-only montage.
- A different character owns each rung. If one character has two rungs in a row, merge them.
- The joke must survive with the sound off. Write the captions as part of the script, not afterwards.

## 2. Channel concept and character bible

### Channel name options

1. **Lesser Pemberton** (recommended)
2. Town Problem
3. Pemberton (Lesser)
4. Concerned Residents
5. Minutes of the Meeting
6. Pop. 4,012 (Pending Review)
7. The Bypass Gazette
8. Second-Best Town
9. Porch Cam
10. Tiny Town Crisis

**Pick: Lesser Pemberton.** It contains the premise (status anxiety), it is ownable, and it works as a handle (@LesserPemberton). Search YouTube, Instagram and TikTok for the handle, and do a basic trademark search in your country, before you design anything.

### Positioning (one sentence)

A 30-second cartoon town where every tiny inconvenience becomes a municipal crisis, and five neighbours make it worse in five different ways.

### Why it is not a clone

| Genre default | Lesser Pemberton |
|---|---|
| Kids as protagonists | All adults, behaving like kids. Youngest cast member is 19. |
| Mountain town, isolated | Commuter strip-mall town cut in half by a bypass, staring at its richer twin across the road |
| Construction-paper cutout look | Risograph print look: two or three flat ink colours with deliberate misregistration, wobbly marker linework |
| Episodic 22-minute stories | 30-second "Town Problem" cases with a numbered stamp |
| Violent recurring death gag | A silent, competent raccoon who quietly fixes or steals whatever the humans are fighting about |

### Target audience and viewing context

- **Who:** 18 to 40, English-speaking, works or studies, lives with subscriptions, group chats, tip screens and a neighbourhood app. Has a Deb in their family and a Marcus in their friend group.
- **Where:** Thumb-scrolling the Shorts feed on a phone, often sound-off in the first second (queue, commute, bed, bathroom, open-plan office). The first frame must read as a joke silently. Captions are burned in, always.
- **Why they share it:** "This is literally my aunt" / "this is my town's Facebook group". Write for the tag-a-friend reflex: each character must be recognisable as a *type of person the viewer knows*.

### Visual identity

**Look:** a cheap community newsletter printed on a risograph that nobody maintains. Flat ink colours, 3 to 6 px colour misregistration between layers, visible paper grain, marker linework that wobbles (draw it with a mouse or cheap tablet on purpose; do not smooth it).

**Palette (riso inks)**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Paper / background base | Newsletter Cream | `#F4EFE4` |
| Linework | Toner Black | `#1E1B1A` |
| Primary accent (Deb, alerts, stamp) | Fluoro Pink | `#FF48B0` |
| Secondary (Lesser Pemberton) | Faded Teal | `#00838A` |
| Highlight (hi-vis, Gord, Lenny's vest) | Riso Yellow | `#FFE800` |
| Greater Pemberton only (shiny, rich, cold) | Federal Blue | `#3D5588` |

Rule: Lesser Pemberton never uses Federal Blue. Greater Pemberton is only ever drawn in Federal Blue, with clean straight lines. The visual contrast is the class joke.

**Background style:** single flat illustrated plates, traced loosely from your own phone photos of real strip malls, car parks and porches near you (your photos, your town = specific details no generator invents). Hand-letter every sign.

**Animation approach:** 2D cutout puppets at 12 fps (animate on twos). Snap between held poses; no smooth tweened floating, no slow motion. Smear frame or motion lines on fast turns. Camera moves are hard cuts and instant punch-ins, never slow drifts. Six mouth shapes from Rhubarb Lip Sync (A to F) plus a rest shape.

**Free tool chain:** Krita (draw puppets and plates) → OpenToonz (cutout rig, lip-sync from Rhubarb's export) → DaVinci Resolve free (edit, sound, captions as text layers). Timing for captions: run the final mix through Whisper or faster-whisper to get an SRT, then paste in your script text, because you already know the words.

**Recurring locations (build all six plates in week one)**

1. **Pemberton Plaza:** half-empty strip mall. Units: Priya's phone-repair kiosk, "Taxes & Tans", ValuMore grocery with self-checkouts, a vape shop called "Cloud Nein".
2. **Deb's porch:** PeepHole Pro doorbell camera, a garden gnome wearing a lanyard, lawn sign that says "WE ARE WATCHING (LOVINGLY)".
3. **Town Hall:** a former pizza restaurant. The roof is still pizza-hut shaped (generic, no logo), the faded sign still reads "PIZZ". Wendy's podium sits where the salad bar was.
4. **Gord's garage and garden shed:** green folding chair, a wall calendar stuck on 1987, a radio playing muffled talk.
5. **The Bypass overpass:** the view of Greater Pemberton's blue glass skyline across four lanes of traffic.
6. **Lenny's dumpster:** behind the plaza, with a tiny municipal traffic cone and a reading lamp.

### The cast

Each character has one silhouette shape so they read at thumbnail size, one desire, one flaw, one verbal habit, and one comic job. Verbal habits have **quotas** (see section 5) so they stay signatures instead of becoming repetitive.

**DEB KOWALCZYK (52), the Alarm**
- Shape: triangle. Big hair, A-line cardigan, phone held at arm's length.
- Job: moderator of "Lesser Pemberton Concerned Residents" on the Porchlight app.
- Wants: to be needed. Flaw: turns any inconvenience into an emergency, then gets bored once it's solved.
- Speech: starts with "Okay so." Dictates posts out loud including punctuation and caps ("THURSDAY, comma, in CAPS"). Calls people "hon" only when furious. Trails off with "...is all I'm saying."
- Comic function: starts the escalation. Turns a private problem public. Priya's aunt.
- Voice direction: fast, slightly breathless, pitch rises at the end of complaints, hard consonants on caps words.

**MARCUS HALE (31), the Monetiser**
- Shape: tall rectangle, puffer vest, two phones.
- Job: "founder". Lives in his mum's garage. Currently pivoting.
- Wants: to be a visionary. Flaw: sees every problem as a revenue stream, cannot feel embarrassment.
- Speech: startup jargon used slightly wrong ("that's a vertical", "we're pre-revenue on the candle line"). Says "Respectfully" before disrespectful sentences. Laughs at his own idea: "heh, okay, okay."
- Comic function: the second rung. Takes whatever is happening and productises it. Turns up from nowhere (out of a hedge, behind a bin).
- Voice direction: confident, upbeat, slightly too loud, speeds up when pitching, pauses before the number.

**PRIYA NANDAKUMAR (19), the Correction**
- Shape: small circle, hood up, giant phone with a cracked screen.
- Job: community college student, works the phone-repair kiosk at the plaza. Deb's niece.
- Wants: to be left alone. Flaw: as addicted to her phone as everyone she judges, and she knows it.
- Speech: short, flat, lowercase energy. States the obvious fact nobody wants: "it's a hole." Asks "who is that for." Never more than eight words per line.
- Comic function: the straight line that makes the absurdity visible. In one Short in four she is the hypocrite (see Short 6), so she never becomes a smug narrator.
- Voice direction: low energy, flat pitch, slight vocal fry is fine, deadpan pauses before she speaks.

**GORD BRANNIGAN (68), the Wrecking Ball**
- Shape: wide trapezoid, hi-vis crossing-guard sash, tube socks to the knee, flip phone.
- Job: retired, volunteers for everything (crossing guard, council seat, bingo caller).
- Wants: things to stay how they were. Flaw: stubbornness that accidentally wins. Thinks 1987 was the last good year.
- Speech: refers to himself as "Gord" when announcing what he is doing ("Gord's not moving"). Tangents that start "In 1987..." and end abruptly with no payoff. Names apps by colour ("the blue one").
- Comic function: the analogue chaos agent. His refusal to play along short-circuits the system, and about half the time he wins by accident.
- Voice direction: slow, warm, gravelly, long pauses mid-sentence, drops volume at the end of tangents.

**COUNCILLOR WENDY STRAND (45), the Institution**
- Shape: square, shoulder pads, always holding oversized ceremonial scissors.
- Job: acting mayor (the real mayor moved to Greater Pemberton).
- Wants: a ribbon to cut. Flaw: solves problems with rebrands, signage, task forces and announcements, never with fixing.
- Speech: press-conference cadence. "Pleased to announce." "Going forward." Applauds herself.
- Comic function: the institutional rung. Makes the absurd thing official.
- Voice direction: bright, projected, every sentence lands like a soundbite, smile in the voice even when the news is bad.

**LENNY (raccoon, age unknown), the Silent Competent One**
- Shape: small, wears a tiny hi-vis vest he found.
- Never speaks. Never breaks the fourth wall more than one look per Short.
- Wants: whatever the humans are fighting over, usually food or a better chair.
- Comic function: the visual punchline and the silent-version anchor. He is the only resident who gets things done. **Quota: Lenny closes at most 3 Shorts in any 10.** Otherwise he becomes the predictable ending.

### Supporting fixtures (no lines or one line)

- **Greater Pemberton:** never seen up close. Glass towers, a smoothie bar visible from the overpass, a council that does "goodwill gestures".
- **Porchlight:** the neighbourhood app. Pink header, bell icon, comments always include someone named "Ron Dellamy (Ward 3)" asking an unrelated question about bins.
- **TopTenTowns.biz:** fictional listicle site that ranks the town last.

### The repeatable format: "Town Problem #___"

Every Short follows this skeleton. The skeleton is what makes production fast; the characters are what stop it looking templated.

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 to 1.5 s | **Hook:** the problem in one image plus one line | Must read with the sound off. No title card. No "POV". |
| 1.5 s | Stamp "TOWN PROBLEM #___" slams top-left | Numbers are deliberately out of order (Short 1 is #212). Fans will comment on it. |
| 1.5 to 8 s | **Goes public:** usually Deb posts it on Porchlight | Show the post on screen, read aloud with Deb's dictated punctuation |
| 8 to 24 s | **Three rungs**, each owned by a different character | But/therefore between every rung |
| 24 to 30 s | **Turn:** reversal, Priya's correction, or Gord accidentally winning | |
| 30 to 34 s | **Tag:** callback, visual punchline, or loop into frame one | Never a "subscribe" ask |

Production target once the six plates and five puppets exist: 3 to 5 hours per Short.

## 4. The first ten scripts

### Script notation

- `DEB:` normal line. `DEB (V.O.):` voice-over.
- `//` marks where the next speaker starts talking over this line. In the edit, start the next clip at that point (usually 150 to 300 ms early).
- `--` means the line is cut off.
- `(beat)` = a 0.4 s pause. `(2 beat)` = 0.8 s.
- Caption colours: Deb pink, Marcus teal, Priya white, Gord yellow, Wendy cream on black, SFX captions in [brackets] only when the sound *is* the joke.
- Every script lists **the point** (never spoken), **the specific detail** (the one thing people will quote), and the **ending type** so endings stay varied.

Ending mix across the ten: loop x2, reversal x4, visual punchline x2, cross-episode callback x2. Lenny closes 3 of 10.

### Short 1: We Saved the Pothole (From Being Fixed)

- **Thumbnail / cover text:** IT'S A LANDMARK NOW
- **The point:** People will defend anything that has become part of their identity, including the problem they complained about.
- **Specific detail:** The pothole is called Barry and has a rubber duck that's been floating in it since 2015.
- **Ending type:** Loop (Lenny).
- **Runtime:** 34 s, 72 spoken words.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 to 1.5 | ECU: Deb, eyes wet, flicking a lighter at a tea-light candle. | **DEB:** Barry's been with us eleven years. SFX: lighter flick (fails once, then lights). Room tone: distant leaf blower. |

### Short 2: We Banned Politics at Dinner. Then Someone Mentioned the Weather.

- **Thumbnail / cover text:** THE WEATHER STARTED IT
- **The point:** Banning a topic doesn't remove the fight; people pour the same anger into whatever topic is left.
- **Specific detail:** Wendy's "bipartisan gravy".
- **Ending type:** Visual punchline.
- **Publish:** Wednesday 25 November, the day before US Thanksgiving. Keep the food generic (turkey, gravy) so it reposts for Christmas.
- **Runtime:** 33 s, 84 spoken words.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 to 1.5 | Deb slaps a laminated card reading RULES onto the turkey. It sticks. | **DEB:** No politics. No phones. SFX: wet laminate slap. |
| 1.5 to 4.0 | Hard cuts, 0.6 s each: Gord, already holding his flip phone under the table; Marcus's two phones face-down; Priya's hand twitching; Wendy smiling. Stamp: TOWN PROBLEM #048. | SFX: stamp slam, then a very loud clock tick, gravy glug. Nobody speaks for 1.2 s. |

## 5. AI-content audit and fixes

Two parts: the generic tells that make a Short read as machine-made, then an honest audit of the scripts above.

### 5a. Common tells and the fix for each

| Tell | How it shows up in a cartoon channel | Concrete fix |
|---|---|---|
| Generic phrasing | Lines anyone could say ("This is getting out of hand!") | Every line must be sayable by *one* character only. Test: cover the name tag; if you can't tell who's speaking, rewrite. |
| Overly clean grammar | Full sentences, no fragments, no false starts | Give each character a grammar fingerprint. Priya: no capitals, fragments. Gord: runs on, then stops dead. Deb: says punctuation aloud. Add one self-interruption per Short ("We're doing hoodies, a candle, a--"). |
| Repetitive sentence rhythm | Every line 8 to 12 words, same cadence | Mix one-word lines ("Lovely.") with one long line per Short. Read the script aloud with a timer: if you fall into a sing-song, cut words until it breaks. |
| Emotionless or over-polished narration | A smooth narrator explaining the joke | **No narrator, ever.** Characters speak; screens and signs carry exposition. |
| Stock-looking characters | Big-eyed, smooth-gradient, "friendly mascot" designs | Hand-draw five puppets once, with a mouse if you must. Wobbly linework, flat riso colours, 3 to 6 px misregistration. Don't generate character art with an image model: consistency drifts and hands melt. |
| Stiff movement | Puppets slide around on smooth tweens | Animate on twos (12 fps), snap between held poses, add one anticipation frame before big moves. Blink at irregular intervals (2 to 7 s), never on a timer. |
| Uncanny faces | Generated faces with too much detail | Dot eyes, six mouth shapes. Stylisation removes the uncanny valley entirely. |
| Slow motion | "Cinematic" slow-mo reactions | Banned. Comedy uses holds: freeze on a pose for 0.8 s instead. |
| Visuals that don't match the line | Generic B-roll of "a town" under dialogue | Every shot must illustrate or contradict the line being spoken. Write picture and sound in the same table row (as in section 4) so you can't drift. |
| Inspirational or "epic" music | Swelling strings under a joke | Music under dialogue is banned. Music appears only as stings (1 to 3 s) and parodies (the choir ding-dong). Room tone does the rest. |
| "Cinematic" empty shots | Establishing shots with nothing happening | Every establishing shot must contain a joke: a sign, a background character, Lenny doing something. |
| Robotic pacing | Even gaps between lines, nobody overlaps | Overlap 2 to 3 lines per Short by 150 to 300 ms (`//` in the script). Vary gaps: 0 ms on arguments, 800 ms before a deadpan answer. |
| Lack of specific detail | "A pothole", "a fridge" | One specific memorable detail per Short (Barry's duck, the timeshare, the door's family). Listed at the top of every script. |
| No opinion | Jokes that observe without taking a side | The script header states "the point" as an opinion. If it reads like a neutral fact, it's not a point. |
| Repetitive hooks | Every Short opens the same way | Rotate hook types (list below). Never use the same type twice in a row. |
| Predictable structure | Viewer knows exactly when the turn comes | Vary where the turn lands (18 s in one, 28 s in another). One Short in five skips the Porchlight post entirely. |
| Weak or generic endings | "Like and subscribe", fade out, a character summarising | Endings are reversal, callback, visual punchline or loop. Track the mix (section 4 header). No call to action, ever, in the video. |
| AI voice flatness | Same intonation contour on every line | Generate 3 to 5 takes per line and pick, splice the best halves, vary speed per line, add breaths and room tone. Full settings in section 7. |
| Captions that look auto-generated | Default white font, whole sentences on screen | Speaker-coloured captions, max 4 words per chunk, hand-picked break points so the punchline word lands alone on screen. |

**Hook types to rotate (one per Short, never two in a row):** ambiguous emotional line (Short 1), object slam (2), screen close-up with a question (3), sound subversion (4), physical struggle (5), speech setup (6), broken-format footage (7), unveiling (8), surveillance footage (9), listing or document (10), mid-argument cold open, one-word answer to a question we didn't hear.

## 6. Human texture checklist (apply to every Short)

Tick every box or fix it before Thursday's QC.

**Dialogue**
- [ ] Every line passes the "cover the name tag" test: only one character could say it.
- [ ] At least one interruption or overlap (`//`) and one cut-off line (`--`).
- [ ] At least one line under three words and one over twelve.
- [ ] At least one pause of 0.8 s or longer before a deadpan answer.
- [ ] Catchphrase quotas respected (Marcus "Respectfully" max 1, Wendy "Going forward" max 1, Gord third-person max 1).
- [ ] No character explains the joke or states the point aloud.

**Performance and sound**
- [ ] Each line was picked from at least 3 voice takes.
- [ ] Breaths kept on at least 2 lines (not cleaned out).
- [ ] Room tone under every scene (plaza HVAC hum, porch leaf blower, garage radio). Silence is never digital zero.
- [ ] One awkward reaction sound (throat clear, sniff, chair creak, late clap).
- [ ] One piece of foley recorded by you on your phone (keys, a mug, a door, a zip). Swap in a new one each week.
- [ ] No music under dialogue. Stings only.

**Picture**
- [ ] Every shot illustrates or contradicts the line spoken over it.
- [ ] Shot lengths vary: at least one shot under 0.7 s and one held over 2.5 s.
- [ ] At least three different framings (ECU, medium, wide).
- [ ] No slow motion, no slow camera drift, no empty establishing shot.
- [ ] One visible imperfection kept on purpose: a wobbly line, a misregistered colour, a background prop that's slightly off.
- [ ] At least one background gag (a sign, Ron Dellamy's bin comment, Lenny doing something).

**Structure**
- [ ] Engine filled in: premise, contradiction, ladder, target, reaction, punchline, point.
- [ ] Every rung joined by "but" or "therefore". No "and then".
- [ ] Hook type differs from the previous Short.
- [ ] One specific memorable detail, named in the script header.
- [ ] Ending is a reversal, callback, visual punchline or loop. Ending type differs from the previous Short.
- [ ] No call to action inside the video.

**Silent pass**
- [ ] Watched muted: the hook, the turn and the ending all read without sound.
- [ ] Punchline word sits alone on screen as its own caption chunk.

### Recommended workflow

**Primary: your own performance + Zonos cloning (free Colab or a 6 GB+ GPU).**

The most human-sounding route is to perform the characters yourself (or with friends who sign a one-line consent note), then use Zonos to turn those performances into five consistent, distinct voices.

1. Record a 20 to 30 s reference clip per character on your phone in a quiet room: you (or a friend) reading a neutral paragraph *in that character's attitude*. Same mic, same room, no music. Five clips total. These are your "voice actors" forever.
2. In the Zonos Gradio interface, load the character's reference clip, paste one line, apply the settings below, generate 3 to 5 takes.
3. Export WAV, name it `S03_L07_GORD_t2.wav`, pick the best take in the edit.
4. Use Piper `en_US-joe-medium` (CC0) for machine characters (self-checkout, doorbell, fridge, door): flat and synthetic on purpose, which makes the humans sound more human by contrast.

**Backup: Kokoro on CPU, with the acting done in the edit.**

Kokoro has no emotion control, so the edit carries the performance: line-by-line generation, `speed` per character, pauses and overlaps built in the timeline, pitch shift in Audacity (Effect > Pitch and Tempo > Change Pitch, which keeps timing), and one take per line split at the best syllable.

**Hosted fallback (no GPU, want SSML):** Google Cloud TTS Neural2 or WaveNet voices under the free monthly allowance, with SSML for pauses and emphasis. Set a $1 budget alert on the billing account the day you create it.

### Exact starting settings

These are starting points from the documented parameter ranges. Tune by ear on the first Short, then freeze them in a text file so every episode sounds like the same cast.

**Zonos** (emotion sliders: Happiness, Sadness, Disgust, Fear, Surprise, Anger, Other, Neutral, each 0 to 1; default speaking rate 15, pitch variation 45, CFG 2)

| Character | Speaking rate | Pitch variation | CFG | Emotion mix | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deb | 19 | 110 | 2.5 | Anger 0.45, Surprise 0.35, Fear 0.2, Neutral 0.1 | For CAPS words, generate that word as its own clip with Anger 0.8 and splice it in |
| Marcus | 18 | 80 | 2.0 | Happiness 0.6, Surprise 0.2, Neutral 0.2 | Drop rate to 14 for the half-second before a number ("...the pothole budget") |
| Priya | 13 | 20 | 2.0 | Neutral 0.8, Disgust 0.15, Sadness 0.05 | Low pitch variation is the deadpan. Never above 30 |
| Gord | 11 | 50 | 1.8 | Neutral 0.5, Happiness 0.3, Sadness 0.2 | Lower CFG lets the pauses wander. Tangents: generate in two halves and leave 0.6 s between them |
| Wendy | 16 | 90 | 3.0 | Happiness 0.7, Neutral 0.3 | Bad-news lines keep Happiness 0.7: the smile while announcing disaster is the joke |

**Kokoro** (`speed`, default 1.0): Deb 1.12, Marcus 1.08, Priya 0.92, Gord 0.85, Wendy 1.0. Audition the preset voices and give each character a voice no other character uses. Post pitch shift in Audacity: Gord -1.5 semitones, Deb +1, others 0. Never shift more than 2 semitones: beyond that, voices start to sound processed.

**Google Cloud SSML** (Wendy example):

```xml
<speak>
  <prosody rate="105%" pitch="+2st">Pleased to announce</prosody>
  <break time="300ms"/>
  Barry is now a
  <emphasis level="strong">protected</emphasis>
  heritage site.
</speak>
```

Priya lines: `<prosody rate="90%" pitch="-2st">`. Gord lines: `<prosody rate="85%">` with `<break time="700ms"/>` mid-sentence before the tangent payoff.

### Dialogue direction rules (any tool)

- **Generate line by line, never whole scenes.** Whole-scene generation flattens everyone into the same energy.
- **Pauses live in the timeline, not the text.** Put the 0.8 s deadpan gap in the edit, where you can trim it by frames.
- **Overlaps live in the timeline.** Slide the interrupting line 150 to 300 ms into the end of the previous one and duck the first line by 4 dB under it.
- **Spell for sound.** "Ell-pay", "heh, okay, okay", "nnnno". Ellipses for hesitation, commas for breath. Don't use CAPS for emphasis inside a TTS prompt unless that model handles it: generate the stressed word separately instead.
- **One filler per character per Short at most** ("uh", "hm"). More sounds written, not performed.
- **Keep breaths** at the start of two or three lines. Don't noise-gate them away.
- **Give each character a mic colour** in Resolve's Fairlight EQ: Gord with a gentle cut above 9 kHz (warm), Priya with a little boost at 200 Hz (close, quiet), Deb with a little boost at 3 kHz (shrill), Wendy dry and bright as if on a PA.
- **Room tone under everything**, at about -45 dBFS, matched to the location.
- **Lenny never speaks.** Not even a squeak. His silence is the character.

### Licence log (keep it from day one)

One spreadsheet row per asset: file, tool and version, licence name, link, date downloaded, screenshot of the licence page, attribution text required. If YouTube or a claimant ever asks, you can answer in two minutes.

## 8. Weekly production workflow

Target: **3 Shorts per week.** One a week is too slow to learn what works. Daily from a solo creator is how you end up with templated slop, which YouTube's inauthentic-content rules demonetise.

Times assume the six location plates and five puppets already exist (budget one setup week for those first).

### Monday: topic and scripts (3 hours)

1. Open the idea bank (section 3) and your "this week" note of things people argued about online. Pick 3: one evergreen, one topical, one character spotlight for whoever has been quiet.
2. Fill the comedy engine table for each before writing dialogue.
3. Write each script in the section 4 table format (time, picture, sound).
4. Read each script aloud with a phone stopwatch. Over 34 s: cut words, not pauses.
5. Run the "cover the name tag" test on every line.
6. Record a scratch read on your phone, doing all the voices badly. This is your timing guide and your performance reference for the TTS.

### Tuesday: voices and visuals (4 to 5 hours)

1. Generate voices line by line (not whole scenes), 3 to 5 takes each, using the settings in section 7. Name files `S03_L07_GORD_t2.wav`.
2. Assemble the dialogue track in Resolve: pick takes, build overlaps, set pauses against your scratch read.
3. Export the dialogue stem and run Rhubarb Lip Sync on each character's lines.
4. Animate in OpenToonz: poses first, lip-sync second, blinks last.
5. Render each scene as a separate clip (easier re-edits).

### Wednesday: captions, sound design, pacing pass (3 to 4 hours)

1. Captions: run Whisper for timing, paste your script text, break into 4-word chunks, speaker colours, punchline word alone.
2. Sound: room tone bed per location, SFX from the script, your own foley item, stings. Mix dialogue to peak around -6 dBFS, final master to about -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP ceiling.
3. Pacing pass: watch at 1x, then cut 10%. Trim frames between lines, shorten any shot where nothing changes. Comedy almost always improves when tighter, except before a deadpan line.
4. Build the loop: check the last frame cuts cleanly into the first.

### Thursday: QC (1.5 hours)

1. Watch every Short on your phone, in the Shorts-shaped player, three times: sound on, sound off, and at a distance (arm's length, glancing).
2. Run the human texture checklist (section 6) and the upload checklist (section 9).
3. Show one Short to one person without explaining it. Watch their face, not the screen. Note the exact second they laugh or look away.
4. Fix anything that fails. If a Short fails the silent pass and can't be fixed in 30 minutes, hold it.

### Friday: publish and log (1 hour)

1. Publish Short A today; schedule B for Sunday and C for Tuesday at the same time of day. Keep the time fixed for four weeks before testing a different slot.
2. Log last week's Shorts at the 7-day mark in a spreadsheet:

| Column | Where in YouTube Studio | My working target |
|---|---|---|
| Views at 7 days | Overview | Trend only |
| Viewed vs swiped away | Shorts metrics in Analytics | 70%+ means the hook works |
| Average percentage viewed | Engagement tab | 90%+; over 100% means people loop |
| Retention dip time | Retention graph | Write the second and what was on screen |
| Subscribers gained | Overview | Trend only |
| Quoted lines in comments | Comments | Copy each one verbatim: this is your best-joke signal |
| Hook type, ending type, featured character | Your script header | For pattern-spotting |

The targets are my working benchmarks to start with, not YouTube figures. Replace them with your own channel median after 15 uploads.

### Weekly review (Friday, 30 minutes)

Answer these five, in writing, in the same document every week:

1. **Best hook:** highest viewed-vs-swiped. What was in frame one?
2. **Best joke:** the line most quoted in comments, or the spot where the retention graph rises (rewinds). Why did it land?
3. **Best retention point:** where the graph is flattest. What kept people?
4. **Weakest video:** lowest average percentage viewed. Find the exact second of the biggest drop and name the cause (slow setup, unclear joke, wrong character, weak hook).
5. **One change next week:** one only. Write it at the top of Monday's script file.

After four weeks, double down on the best-performing character pairing and the best hook type, but keep the rotation rules: a channel that repeats its winner every time becomes the repetitive content YouTube demonetises.

## 9. Final quality-control checklist before upload

**Technical**
- [ ] 1080 x 1920, 9:16, 24 or 30 fps (consistent across the channel), H.264, high bitrate export.
- [ ] Runtime under 60 s (this format's sweet spot is 20 to 40 s).
- [ ] Captions and key visuals inside the safe zone: nothing important in the bottom 20% (title and buttons) or the right-hand 15% (like, comment, share icons).
- [ ] Loudness about -14 LUFS integrated, true peak under -1 dBTP. No clipping on Deb's shouting lines.
- [ ] First frame is the hook image, not black, not a title card.
- [ ] Last frame cuts cleanly into frame one if the Short is meant to loop.

**Content and legal**
- [ ] No real people's names, likenesses or voices. No obvious stand-ins.
- [ ] No real brand names, logos, app icons or store designs on screen.
- [ ] No factual claims about the real world presented as fact.
- [ ] Target is a behaviour or institution, not an identity group.
- [ ] Every voice comes from a tool whose licence permits commercial use on your current plan (see section 7), with any required attribution in the description.
- [ ] Every SFX and music file has a licence you've logged (source, licence, attribution text).
- [ ] Altered or synthetic content setting: YouTube's help page says unrealistic content such as animation does not need the disclosure. If you ever add realistic footage or a realistic synthetic voice of a real person (don't), you must tick it.

**Comedy**
- [ ] Human texture checklist complete (section 6).
- [ ] Watched muted: it still works.
- [ ] One person watched it cold and laughed at least once, or you know which beat failed and why.
- [ ] The hook type and ending type differ from the previous upload.

**Metadata**
- [ ] Title: under 50 characters, states the situation, contains no clickbait the video doesn't pay off. Use the "safe but sharp" titles from section 3.
- [ ] Description: one line of premise, voice/SFX/music credits required by licences, then "Town Problem #___" for the running joke.
- [ ] Cover frame: a frame with the thumbnail text burned in, chosen in the upload flow.
- [ ] Pinned comment: a line from the town (a Porchlight post from Deb, or Ron Dellamy asking about bins). Never "what did you think?".
- [ ] Added to the "Town Problems" playlist.
